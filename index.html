Section 01
Core Concepts
What Is an Antibiotic?

A substance originally derived from bacteria or fungi, chemically modified and used to inhibit or kill bacteria.

⚡ KEY DISTINCTION
Bactericidal = kills bacteria (β-lactams, aminoglycosides, quinolones, metronidazole)
Bacteriostatic = inhibits growth (macrolides, tetracyclines, clindamycin, sulfonamides)
Quick Rules of Thumb
Gram +ve → Penicillins (1st choice)
Gram –ve → 3rd Gen Cephalosporins / Aminoglycosides
Atypicals → Macrolides / Doxycycline
Anaerobes → Metronidazole
4 Universal Side Effect Categories
① Anaphylactic
Urticaria, skin rashes, anaphylactic shock
⚠️ CLINICAL NOTE
Routine "test doses" without an allergy history are not supported by evidence. For patients with a documented allergy, perform formal risk-stratification (e.g., PEN-FAST) or skin testing.
② Irritative (GI)
Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
Erythromycin: worst offender (GI motility ↑)
③ Superinfection
C. difficile → Pseudomembranous colitis
Candidiasis (oral / vaginal)
Most common with broad-spectrum ABx
④ Drug-Specific Toxicity
Each antibiotic has a unique toxic profile
See per-drug sections below
MOA 01
Cell Wall / Membrane Inhibitors
01
Penicillins
β-Lactam ring → inhibits transpeptidase → blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking → cell lysis
⚡ β-LACTAMASE PROBLEM
S. aureus produces β-lactamase → destroys the β-lactam ring → resistance.
Fix: Add β-lactamase inhibitor: Clavulanic acid (Augmentin), Sulbactam, Tazobactam (Tazocin).

⚠️ CROSS-ALLERGY
Penicillin allergy → 10% cross-reactivity with Cephalosporins (classical teaching). True incidence with modern cephalosporins ~1–2%. If prior anaphylaxis to PCN, avoid all β-lactams and use Aztreonam (Gram –ve) or Vancomycin (Gram +ve).

🔬 CEPHALOSPORIN CROSS-ALLERGY RULE
True cross-reactivity with Penicillin allergy: ~1–2% only (was historically overestimated at 10%). For 3rd/4th gen, risk is <1%. Safe to use in most PCN-allergic patients unless prior anaphylaxis to PCN — in that case use Aztreonam (Gram –ve) or Vancomycin (Gram +ve).

Ertapenem
Covers ▶ Gram +ve Gram –ve Anaerobes — NO Pseudomonas / Acinetobacter / Enterococcus ("APE")
 IV / IM Gram +ve (not MRSA/Enterococcus), Gram –ve Enterobacteriaceae, Anaerobes · NO Pseudomonas, NO Acinetobacter Community-acquired ESBL infections (UTI, IAI), diabetic foot, polymicrobial wound infections — when Pseudomonas is unlikely ⚠️ APE mnemonic — lacks Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas, Enterococcus coverage · Advantage: once-daily dosing (long t½) · Can be given IM (unlike others)
Doripenem
Covers ▶ Gram +ve All Gram –ve Pseudomonas ✔✔ Anaerobes

⚠️ CARBAPENEM RESISTANCE — KPC / MBL
Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase (KPC) and metallo-β-lactamases (NDM, VIM, IMP) hydrolyse carbapenems. Options: Colistin, Ceftazidime-Avibactam, Cefiderocol. Refer immediately to ID specialist.
Vancomycin (Glycopeptide · D-Ala-D-Ala binding)
Covers ▶ Gram +ve ONLY MRSA / VRSA C. diff (oral) — NO Gram –ve activity
1st line MRSA; oral form for C. diff
⚡ RED MAN SYNDROME
Too-rapid infusion → flushing, hypotension, rash. Not allergy — slow the drip, dilute properly.
☠️ TOXICITY
Nephrotoxic + Ototoxic. Monitor trough levels and creatinine.

✏️ CORRECTED — MYASTHENIA-LIKE SYNDROME MECHANISM
Aminoglycosides block presynaptic ACh release at the NMJ — NOT by binding ACh receptors (that is the mechanism of MG itself and competitive NM blockers like tubocurarine).
Result: ↓ ACh released → muscle weakness → respiratory paralysis if pre-existing RF or MG.
🚫 ABSOLUTE C/I
Respiratory failure · Myasthenia Gravis · Pregnancy (ototoxicity to fetus)

Tetracyclines → yellow-brown to grey discoloration (NOT bluish).

✏️ CORRECTED — GRAY BABY SYNDROME MECHANISM
NOT a bilirubin problem. Chloramphenicol accumulates in neonates because:
(1) Immature hepatic UDP-glucuronosyltransferase → cannot conjugate/inactivate drug
(2) Immature renal excretion → further drug buildup
Result: toxic levels → mitochondrial protein synthesis inhibition → ↓ cardiac output, metabolic acidosis, ashen-grey colour, cardiovascular collapse → death within 48 h.

MOA 05
Free Radical DNA Damage
05
Metronidazole (Flagyl)
Prodrug → enters anaerobic/protozoal cells → reduced to toxic nitro-radical → DNA strand breaks → bactericidal
Covers ▶ All Anaerobes Protozoa (Giardia, Entamoeba, Trichomonas) C. diff (oral)
⚡ WHY ONLY ANAEROBES?
Aerobic bacteria and human cells do NOT reduce the drug to its active toxic form — only anaerobic and microaerophilic organisms do. This is what gives Metronidazole its selective toxicity.
Organism / IndicationClinical Use
Anaerobes (1st line)Intra-abdominal infections, pelvic sepsis, aspiration pneumonia, dental infections
Clostridium difficile⚠️ No longer 1st-line (IDSA 2021). Preferred agents are fidaxomicin > oral vancomycin. Metronidazole only if both are unavailable (non-severe CDI only).
Helicobacter pyloriTriple / quadruple therapy for peptic ulcer
ProtozoaGiardia lamblia, Entamoeba histolytica (amoebic dysentery/liver abscess), Trichomonas vaginalis
🍺 DISULFIRAM-LIKE REACTION
Avoid alcohol during AND 48 h after treatment.
Alcohol + Metronidazole → acetaldehyde buildup → severe flushing, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, tachycardia.
⚠️ OTHER SE / C/I
Metallic taste · Peripheral neuropathy (prolonged use)
C/I: 1st trimester pregnancy · Caution with anticoagulants (↑ warfarin effect)
✏️ UPDATED — C. DIFF GUIDELINE CHANGE (IDSA 2021)
Metronidazole is no longer recommended as first-line for C. difficile infection in adults. Current preference: Fidaxomicin > Oral Vancomycin > Metronidazole (metronidazole only if fidaxomicin and vancomycin are unavailable, non-severe CDI only).
